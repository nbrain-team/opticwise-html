Why Digital Infrastructure is the Backbone of Modern Commercial Real Estate

Discover why digital infrastructure is the backbone of modern commercial real estate. Learn how connectivity, data, and smart tech drive efficiency and value.

March 10, 2025 · 3 min read

TL;DR: Digital infrastructure decides what every other CRE system can do. Owner-controlled networks have become the new backbone of real estate — the layer that determines how much value AI, IoT, and analytics can actually create on top. Backbone weakness shows up in every system above it.

The Brains Behind the Building

In commercial real estate, location is no longer a deal breaker—connectivity is. A prime address means little if tenants struggle with slow Wi-Fi, outdated systems, or inefficient building operations. When a space can’t keep up with modern technology demands, tenants look elsewhere.

Today’s Businesses require smart, efficient, and future-proof spaces that seamlessly integrate high-speed internet, IoT, AI-powered security, and automated energy management. Buildings that lack strong connectivity and smart systems risk higher vacancies and lower property values. This is why digital infrastructure is now the foundation of modern commercial real estate. In fact, according to a recent study by WiredScore, 90% of businesses say reliable digital infrastructure is a key factor in leasing decisions,

Strong digital infrastructure keeps buildings running smoothly, lowers costs, and makes spaces more attractive to businesses—so they are not just nice to have— they’re essential.

In this blog, we’ll break down why digital infrastructure is now the backbone of commercial real estate and how property owners can stay ahead of an evolving market.

Just as steel and concrete provide structural support, digital infrastructure powers modern commercial spaces. Digital infrastructure refers to the technology backbone that powers commercial properties. This includes:

  • High-speed internet and fiber-optic networks for seamless connectivity.
  • Wireless networks and distributed antenna systems (DAS) for strong, uninterrupted signals.
  • Smart building management systems that automate heating, cooling, lighting, and security.
  • IoT-enabled sensors that monitor and optimize building performance in real-time.

In the past, landlords focused on physical upgrades—nicer lobbies, better parking, or modernized office layouts. Today, investments are shifting toward technological enhancements that improve operational efficiency and tenant experience.

Landlords investing in faster networks, automated systems, and digital security gain a competitive edge. Without these upgrades, buildings risk becoming obsolete. A CBRE report revealed that tech-enabled buildings see 5-10% higher occupancy rates compared to traditional buildings. it’s the new foundation that keeps commercial properties competitive, valuable, and future-ready.

Why “Plug-and-Play” Spaces Win the Lease Game

With more companies relying on cloud computing, video conferencing, and remote collaboration tools, a weak digital foundation can cripple productivity. Employees need seamless connectivity whether they’re in the office, at home, or switching between the two. Buildings that lack high-speed internet and robust wireless networks are quickly falling behind.

The days of tenants setting up their own IT infrastructure are over. Businesses want plug-and-play spaces where internet, security, and smart building systems are already in place. Tenants want a fully connected, ready-to-use environment from day one. For landlords, the message is clear: invest in connectivity, or risk losing tenants to smarter buildings.

Investing in digital infrastructure is also a cost-saving strategy for property owners. Heating, cooling, and lighting account for a significant portion of a building’s expenses. Smart building management systems use sensors and automation to adjust energy usage in real-time, cutting waste and lowering costs. The U.S. Department of Energy reports that smart energy systems can reduce energy costs by up to 30%.

Traditional maintenance is reactive—fixing issues after they cause disruptions. Smart HVAC systems can identify inefficiencies and schedule maintenance proactively, extending equipment lifespan and lowering costs. Buildings equipped with fiber internet, automated systems, and AI-driven energy management command higher lease rates and increase in value over time. A JLL report found that smart buildings can achieve 8-10% higher asset value compared to traditional properties.

Future-Proofing Commercial Real Estate

Future-proofing commercial properties means investing in digital infrastructure that can adapt to new innovations and tenant demands. AI-powered building management systems are becoming the norm. Automated climate control, predictive maintenance, and smart security systems enhance efficiency while reducing costs. Investing in scalable, upgradable technology ensures a property remains competitive as new advancements emerge.

Sustainability is now a requirement and is becoming a key factor for tenants and investors. Many are looking for buildings that use less energy and water to lower costs and reduce environmental impact. Upgrades like automated energy management and water-saving systems help cut waste while keeping expenses down. digital infrastructure isn’t just about keeping up—it’s about staying ahead.

In today’s market, strong digital infrastructure is as essential as location and design. Buildings that offer fast, reliable connectivity, smart automation, and energy-efficient systems attract tenants, reduce costs, and maintain long-term value.

Investing in these upgrades isn’t just about keeping up with trends—it’s about ensuring commercial properties remain competitive and profitable in a rapidly evolving landscape. Landlords who prioritize digital infrastructure today will be the ones leading the market tomorrow.
