Unlocking Value: The Power of Data Collection in CRE

Discover how effective data collection can transform commercial real estate operations and enhance asset value, ensuring peak performance.

September 3, 2025 · By Bill Douglas & Drew Hall

In the fast-evolving landscape of commercial real estate (CRE), understanding the true value of data collection is akin to finding gold in a digital mine. This isn’t about jumping on the latest tech trends; it’s about building a robust foundation that transforms properties into performance powerhouses. The mantra is simple: "If you don't own your data & digital infrastructure, your vendors do." In this episode of the Peak Property Performance® podcast, we delve into the third phase of our PPP 5C™ framework: Collect. We explore how effective data collection not only enhances operational efficiency but also significantly boosts asset value.

Understanding the Importance of Data Collection

As we dive into the third C—Collect—our focus shifts to capturing the right data from the right systems. This isn’t just about gathering information; it’s about transforming that data into actionable insights that drive peak property performance. As Bill Douglas puts it, "You want to capture the right data from the right places so that you can stop guessing and start optimizing." This is crucial for CRE owners who aim to enhance both the efficiency and the tenant experience in their properties.

Drew Hall emphasizes the role of people in this phase. "Collect, you have to collect the right people from your organization to help you make this happen," he explains. This means assembling a team that is not only capable but also strategically aligned with the data collection goals. It’s a collective effort where each member plays a vital role in the process, from data identification to implementation.

We’re not just talking about isolated data points; it’s about a comprehensive approach where every system, from building automation to security, contributes to a larger data ecosystem. The goal is to leverage critical data that can lead to substantial benefits, such as improved operational efficiency and increased tenant satisfaction.

Strategies for Data Storage and Management

Once the data is collected, the next challenge lies in deciding where and how to store it. This decision is pivotal because it impacts the accessibility and usability of the data. Drew raises a critical point: "Are we going to store it jointly with a vendor? Are we going to use a cloud provider? Are we going to use our own hardware?" These questions need careful consideration as they dictate the data's future utility and security.

Bill highlights the importance of a well-thought-out strategy: "Collect as much as possible because it's easier to not use than it is to, oops, I wish I had that." The idea is to gather a broad spectrum of data initially, which allows for greater flexibility in the future. This approach not only facilitates immediate insights but also lays the groundwork for advanced analytics, such as machine learning.

The episode also touches on the concept of a data lake—a central repository that allows for storing structured and unstructured data at any scale. By owning this repository, property owners can ensure that they control their data destiny, rather than leaving it in the hands of external vendors.

Real-World Examples of Data-Driven Decisions

To illustrate the power of data collection, the hosts share a compelling case study involving a mixed-use commercial property. The facility faced an issue with an anchor restaurant tenant's audio-visual system. Thanks to the interconnected systems and comprehensive data collection, the root cause was identified swiftly, minimizing downtime and enhancing tenant satisfaction.

As Drew recounts, "It was the confluence of data from multiple systems that contributed to understanding why this even happened." This scenario underscores the value of a connected data infrastructure that allows for seamless troubleshooting and quick resolutions, thus maintaining high operational standards.

Bill adds, "Having this data drives the asset value of your facility and your portfolio." This is not just theoretical; properties with well-documented data histories attract premium prices because they reduce perceived risks for potential buyers. It’s a clear testament to the financial benefits of owning and managing your data effectively.

For more insights and in-depth discussion, you can listen to the full episode.

Real-World Examples of Data-Driven Decisions

The power of data collection becomes evident when we examine real-world scenarios where connected systems transform operational challenges into opportunities for improvement. Recently, one of our commercial clients faced an unexpected issue with an anchor restaurant tenant. The restaurant's audio-visual system experienced a network interruption, affecting not only their operations but also the surrounding systems on the property.

Bill Douglas explained, "It was the confluence of data from multiple systems that contributed to understanding why this even happened." The interconnectedness of these systems allowed OpticWise to trace the root cause efficiently, showcasing the importance of a comprehensive data ecosystem. Without this interconnected data landscape, isolating the problem would have been significantly more challenging.

Drew Hall added, "The side benefit was system resilience and uptime because it was redundant network monitoring." This scenario underscores the value of not just collecting data but understanding and utilizing it to enhance system resilience and tenant satisfaction. It’s a vivid demonstration of how data-driven insights can lead to immediate, tangible benefits.

Automation, System Resilience, and Future Trends

As data collection becomes more sophisticated, automation and advanced analytics like machine learning enter the fray, enhancing CRE operations significantly. Bill noted, "Over time, as more data is pumped into the system and you can apply machine learning, the possibilities increase greatly in terms of what you can do in response to those amalgamation of events."

This capability allows property owners to shift from reactive to proactive management. By integrating machine learning algorithms, predictive maintenance becomes possible, reducing downtime and enhancing operational efficiency. The future of CRE lies in harnessing these technologies to stay ahead of potential issues before they impact tenants.

Moreover, as Drew pointed out, "Collecting data is only the beginning; understanding and applying it strategically is where the magic happens." The conversation around data storage and management emphasizes the need for secure and accessible data lakes, which serve as repositories for all collected data, ready for analysis and decision-making.

Conclusion and Next Steps in the 5C Process

The journey through the Collect phase of the PPP 5C™ framework opens the door to the next step: Coordinate. As we discussed in this episode, collecting data is not an end in itself but a means to facilitate better coordination and control across your property portfolio. "Collect is sort of a means to an end," Bill remarked, emphasizing the transition from mere data accumulation to strategic action.

Looking ahead, the focus shifts to how CRE owners can leverage collected data to enhance coordination among systems and stakeholders. This means creating a seamless flow of information that empowers all parties involved in property management, from operators to tenants, to make informed decisions.

For those keen to dive deeper, we recommend exploring our Peak Property Performance® book, which offers a comprehensive guide to implementing these strategies. Additionally, tune into our Peak Property Performance® Podcast for more insights and stories from industry leaders.

Actionable Takeaways for CRE Owners

As we conclude this episode, let's distill our discussion into actionable steps for CRE owners looking to harness the power of data:

  • Invest in Data Infrastructure: Ensure your property is equipped with the necessary digital infrastructure to collect and manage data effectively.
  • Build a Strategic Team: Assemble a team that understands both the technical and operational aspects of data management to drive strategic decisions.
  • Embrace Automation: Leverage automation and machine learning to enhance operational efficiency and reduce downtime across your properties.
  • Secure Your Data: Implement robust data storage solutions, such as data lakes, to ensure secure and accessible data for future analysis.
  • Coordinate and Control: Use the collected data to improve coordination among systems and stakeholders, driving enhanced property performance.

By taking these steps, you can transform your properties into high-performing assets, ready to meet the demands of a digital future.
